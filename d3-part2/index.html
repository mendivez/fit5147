History of live performances in Melbourne

AusStage is a database of national live performances, a project led by multiple universities, government agencies, industry partners and funded by the Australian Research Council (ARC) among others. It is a multi-source aggregation and maintenance effort. As a result, coverage can be biased since certain venues that are more active in their contributions can be over-represented. This time we focus on live performances in metropolitan Melbourne and their evolution, but keep in mind that any trend described in this narrative could be because of an organic growth of events over time, but also due to improvements in recent record-keeping.

Evolution of Melbourne events

In the visualisation below, we observe that the scope of the data are events that run from 1900 to 2025. We limit the data for technical purposes but AusStage actually contains records that date back to the 1800s. The data also considers venues that host these events and we can see that being represented in the map below, as an aggregation per suburb. Both the chart and the map only include events held in the suburbs shown on the map (about 87% of AusStage's Victorian events since 1900).

The chart below shows active events, with an event being an ongoing production that spans a certain period of time (months, years) rather than a one-night presentation. We can see the peak of active events occurring between 2005 and 2015 with a sharp decline at the start of the 2010s, recovering a bit before plummeting down to almost zero in 2020 due to the COVID-19 global pandemic.

The map shows active events per Melbourne suburb as a percentage of the total from the time selection window in the line chart. It is probably no surprise that suburbs in the vicinity of Melbourne's CBD always dominated in terms of venue choices for live performances (ever since the 1900s). Until perhaps the 1980s, most events were conducted in the CBD and the eastern suburbs, consistently expanding to the north east. Slowly though, performances started to become more popular in the outer suburbs, expanding to the west and creating a bit of a performance hub in the suburb of Werribee (from the early 2000s until today).

Conclusion

The AusStage database is, to an extent, a community-maintained, decentralised repository of live performances in Australia. It is worth noting that venues, organisations, private companies, volunteers all make contributions in different volumes, creating a rich, but perhaps uneven, snapshot of live performances across Australia. It is important to acknowledge the historical significance of these types of events and the maintainers that make this registration possible.

Ignoring the distribution of active events over the years, we can agree that, from a spatial distribution perspective, performances see the benefit of decentralisation, with more outer suburb venues hosting more events in recent years, even if the overall active count seems to have dropped in the 2020s.
